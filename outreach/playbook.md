# Field Playbook

What to say when someone actually replies. Campaigns do not die at the sending stage. They
die in the third minute of the first meeting, when an official asks a reasonable question and
gets a vague answer.

Everything below is either answerable from verified facts, or flagged as a gap. Nothing is
invented. Antonio ruled 2026-09-02 to start now rather than wait for a perfect brief, so the
remaining gaps are marked by when they actually bite, not treated as blockers.

---

# PART 0. THE POSITION YOU ARE NEGOTIATING FROM

Before the questions, know where you stand.

**N.J.S.A. 40:55D-66.1.** A community residence for the developmentally disabled housing **six
or fewer people, excluding resident staff, is a permitted use in every residential district in
New Jersey**, held to the same requirements as a single family house. Group homes are also named
in the Municipal Land Use Law as an inherently beneficial use.

**You are not asking a town for permission. You are asking for a building.**

Three rules for using this.

1. **Never say it as a threat.** "We do not need your approval" ends the relationship and buys
   you nothing. Its value is internal: you negotiate from a settled position instead of a
   hopeful one, and it shows in how you sit in the room.
2. **Use it only if zoning is raised as an obstacle**, and then gently:
   > My understanding is that a six bed community residence is a permitted use in residential
   > zones under state law, on the same footing as a single family house. I am not here about
   > zoning. I am here because you may have a building that is not doing anything.
3. **Verify with counsel before you say it once.** It is Level B, and it depends on the home
   being licensed as a community residence rather than an intermediate care facility. Both
   caveats are in `schedule.md`. If counsel confirms, this is the strongest fact in the campaign.

**Design at six residents.** Seven pushes the project into conditional use, a planning board,
and roughly three extra months the schedule does not have.

# PART 1. THE QUESTIONS YOU WILL BE ASKED

## 1. "Will this lower property values?"

**This is the question. Everything else is a warm up.**

Do not cite a study. There is research on this, but no source was reachable from this
environment and a half remembered statistic that gets checked will end the meeting.

**Answer with your own record instead, which is stronger anyway:**

> We have operated in [TOWN] since [YEAR]. We run 27 facilities in 18 New Jersey towns, and
> the oldest has been open since 1983. We own 23 of those 27 buildings, so we are not a
> tenant who leaves when it gets hard. Before you take my word for it, call the administrator
> in Closter. We have been on Blanch Avenue there for 43 years. Ask them what kind of
> neighbor we have been.

**Why this works.** It converts an argument you cannot win with data you do not have into an
invitation to check a reference you know is good. An official who makes that call is now
invested.

> [VERIFY, WORTH DOING] There is published research on group homes and property values. Get
> a real citation from your counsel or from The Arc of New Jersey and add it here. Until
> then, the reference answer above stands on its own.

## 2. "Is this a nursing home? An institution?"

> No. It is a group home. Our group homes are six bedroom houses on residential streets. We
> run one intermediate care facility, in Closter, and that is a different kind of building
> entirely. What we are proposing here is a house.

From the facility file, Spectrum's group homes run roughly 2,750 to 4,050 square feet with
six or seven bedrooms. That is a large house, not a facility, and saying so plainly defuses
the mental picture the official is carrying.

## 3. "How many people? How much traffic? Parking?"

Answer from the portfolio, then commit to specifics later.

> Our group homes are typically six bedrooms. Staff work in shifts, so there is vehicle
> movement at shift changes, and no visitor traffic of the kind a clinic generates.

> [ASK ANTONIO, BEFORE ANY MEETING] For the $1 million high needs medical group home
> specifically: how many residents, how many staff on the largest shift, how many parking
> spaces, and what is the delivery and medical transport pattern? These are the first three
> questions a planning board asks and you cannot answer them from the facility list.

## 4. "What happens if Spectrum goes under?"

> We were incorporated in 1986. Our annual budget is $48,907,538. Charity Navigator rates us
> four stars, its highest rating. We own 23 of our 27 buildings outright. We have opened
> facilities in every decade since the 1980s and closed none that I am aware of.

> [VERIFY WITH ANTONIO] Has Spectrum ever closed a facility? If yes, know the story before
> someone else finds it. If no, say so plainly, it is a strong line.

Then offer the structural answer, which is what they are really asking:

> If it would help, we can discuss a reverter clause, so the property returns to the town if
> it ever stops being used for this purpose. We are asking for a long term commitment and we
> are willing to be held to it.

**Offering the reverter before they ask for it is the single most disarming move available.**
It costs Spectrum nothing if Spectrum intends to stay, and it removes the town's largest
fear.

## 5. "Why do you need our land? You have a $49 million budget."

> That budget is operations. It is Medicaid funding that pays for nursing, direct care staff,
> and clinical services for about 600 people a year. It is spent as it arrives. Capital is a
> different problem, and we raise it separately. The Chinery Foundation has committed
> $750,000 to one of our apartment projects, and Columbia Bank has funded our roofs and
> systems since 2018.

> [ASK ANTONIO] Is that characterisation of the budget accurate? Confirm before it is said
> out loud. If Spectrum holds capital reserves, this answer needs rewriting, and an official
> who suspects otherwise will ask directly.

## 6. "Would our own residents get priority?"

**Do not guess at this. It is the question a mayor cares about most and a wrong answer is
worse than no answer.**

> [ASK ANTONIO, BEFORE YOUR FIRST REAL MEETING] How are placements determined? Is it state or DDD assignment, does
> Spectrum have any say, and can a town's own residents be prioritised in any lawful way? A
> town giving up land will ask what its residents get, and "I will find out" is an acceptable
> answer once, in the first meeting, and not twice.

## 7. "Do you pay property taxes?"

**Answered 2026-09-02.** Spectrum is a nonprofit, and Antonio has ruled this is not a
consideration on Spectrum's side.

**It is still a consideration on theirs.** A town looking at a tax exempt use is looking at a
ratable coming off the roll, and someone in the room will say so. Name it before they do:

> We are a nonprofit, so you should assume the property comes off the tax roll. What you get
> instead is a building maintained by an owner who has held property in this county for more
> than forty years, six residents who become your neighbours, and staff jobs. If the ratable
> question is the sticking point, say so and we will talk about what else we can offer.

Do not pretend the loss is not real. Naming it first is what makes the rest credible.

> [ASK ANTONIO, NOT BLOCKING] If a town pushes hard on ratables, is Spectrum open to a payment
> in lieu of taxes, or a services in kind arrangement? Worth knowing your answer before someone
> asks for it.

# PART 2. WHAT TO DO WITH EACH REPLY

## Records request replies

| Reply | Read | Next move | Within |
|---|---|---|---|
| All three documents | Responsive town, real facts in hand | Variant A to the administrator, bracket filled with their own numbers | 3 days |
| Property list only | Land conversation open | Variant A, lead with a named parcel | 3 days |
| Housing plan only | They plan, hold little land | Trust fund and partnership ask, not a site ask | 1 week |
| "Complete our form" | Normal, not a brush off | Complete same day | Same day |
| "What is this for?" | Curiosity, which is good | Short honest answer, then the 20 minute ask | Same day |
| Nothing after 14 days | Wrong person, or unresponsive | One call, then deprioritise | Day 14 |

## Cold email replies

| Reply | Read | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| "Sure, let us talk" | Best case | Send `meeting_agenda.md` the day before. Do not improvise the meeting. |
| "Send me more information" | Soft yes, or a polite stall | Send `capability_onepager.md` **and** propose two specific times. Information without a time slot goes nowhere. |
| "We have no surplus property" | Honest no on land | Pivot in the same reply to the trust fund and to a partnership. Do not vanish. |
| "Talk to [name]" | A referral, the most valuable reply | Log as a new contact, verify, and open with "[name] suggested I contact you." |
| "Not now" | A real answer | Thank them, ask when to come back, set the date in the tracker. |
| Silence | Unknown | Follow the three touch cadence, then stop. |

**A referral outperforms everything else in this campaign.** So ask for one in every single
conversation, including the ones that go badly:

> Even if this is not for [TOWN], who else should I be talking to?

## After a meeting

Thank you email within 24 hours, using `templates/thank_you_email.md`. Antonio approves it
first. The email restates what was agreed, because whatever is written down within a day is
what happened.

# PART 3. ESCALATION LADDER

Use in order. Never skip to the top.

1. **Email.** Three touches, then stop.
2. **Phone.** One call using `phone_script.md`.
3. **Sideways.** Different door, same building. Mayor silent, try the administrator. Both
   silent, try the clerk. Log as new contacts, not as a fourth touch.
4. **Warm introduction.** A board member, a funder, or a Spectrum client already seated on a
   Bergen County advisory board. See the warm introduction audit in `operating_plan.md`.
5. **The CEO letter.** Salvador Moran, on letterhead, by post. Rare, and it stops working
   once it becomes routine.
6. **The open house.** Invite them to the Closter ICF. See `templates/open_house_invite.md`.
   Some people will never answer an email and will absolutely turn up to something.

# PART 4. RULES THAT DO NOT BEND IN A LIVE CONVERSATION

Pressure is when the fabrication happens. These hold in a meeting, on a call, and in a reply
written at 6pm.

1. **Never state a figure you are not certain of.** Say "I do not want to give you a wrong
   number, let me confirm it and email you today." Then do it. It builds more credibility
   than a confident guess and costs nothing.
2. **Never say a Spectrum home earns the town affordable housing credit.** Unverified. It is
   the most tempting sentence in this campaign and it is not yet true on the record.
3. **Never state what Spectrum pays in rent.** Ruled 2026-08-31.
4. **Never name a parcel Spectrum has not seen.**
5. **Never commit to a timeline in a meeting.** The 2027 date is under review. See
   `operating_plan.md`.
6. **Write down what you promised, the same day, in the tracker.** The follow up email must
   match what was actually said.

## THE REMAINING GAPS, AND WHY THEY NO LONGER BLOCK

Antonio ruled 2026-09-02: **start now, do not be too choosy.** That is the right call. Waiting
for a perfect brief costs weeks the deadline does not have, and most of these only matter once
someone is actually interested.

**Closed.** Property tax status, question 7. Spectrum is a nonprofit and this is not a
consideration on Spectrum's side.

**Answer before your first real meeting, not before your first email:**

1. **Resident count, staffing, and parking.** Question 3. Design at six, per Part 0. That
   answer alone covers most of it.
2. **Placement rules and local priority.** Question 6. The one a mayor actually cares about.
   "I will find out" is acceptable once, not twice.
3. **Whether Spectrum has ever closed a facility.** Question 4. Ten minutes to check, and you
   do not want to learn the answer in a meeting.
4. **Whether the operations characterisation of the budget is accurate.** Question 5.

None of these blocks a records request, a verification call, or a first email. Send those
today.
