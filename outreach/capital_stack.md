How the new home gets paid for. Written 2026-09-18 in response to a foundation prospecting
playbook Antonio supplied the same day.

Read `schedule.md` first. This file assumes its two confirmed facts: occupancy by
31 December 2027, and $1 million that has to cover acquisition and renovation together.

## THE SHORT VERSION

The playbook aims at foundations and targets roughly $350,000. There is a state program that
funds this exact project, acquisition and rehabilitation of an existing house for adults with
developmental disabilities, at up to **$500,000 per property**, and `grant_history.md` already
established that **Spectrum has never applied to it.**

Foundations are the fourth source in the stack, not the first. Run them, but run them second,
and do not let a 90 day foundation sprint consume the 105 days that site control needs.

## FINDING 1: THE STATE HAS A PROGRAM BUILT FOR THIS EXACT PROJECT

**The Special Needs Housing Partnership Loan Program, SNHPLP.** NJHMFA, the Department of
Community Affairs, and the Department of Human Services Division of Developmental Disabilities
jointly provide financing to create permanent supportive housing and community residences
**for individuals with developmental disabilities.**

The terms, as returned by search and attributed to the published NJHMFA guidelines:

| Term | What search returned |
|---|---|
| Eligible use | Acquisition **and** rehabilitation of existing three to four bedroom single family houses, and first floor three to four bedroom condominiums |
| Per bed limit | Not more than **$125,000 per bed** |
| Per property cap | **$500,000 per property** |
| What counts toward the cap | The NJHMFA and DCA contribution **plus municipal Affordable Housing Trust Fund dollars and other funds available to the municipality** |
| Timing condition | Acquisition and **all** rehabilitation completed within **six months of mortgage closing** |

> **Level B.** Search attributed to `nj.gov/dca/hmfa/developers/docs/supportivehousing/sn_housing_partnership_loan_guidelines.pdf`.
> nj.gov remains blocked from this environment and I could not open the PDF. Not usable in
> outreach under campaign rule 2 until confirmed by phone or by an opened document.

### Four consequences, in order of size

**1. It funds the only path that fits the schedule.** `schedule.md` ruled that Path A1, an
existing house, is the single path with slack against 31 December 2027, and that new
construction and change of use are both already past their site control dates. SNHPLP funds
acquisition and rehabilitation of an existing house. It does not fund the paths that were
ruled out anyway. The program and the schedule agree.

**2. $500,000 is half the project.** Against a $1 million all in budget, one application to one
state program covers half. No foundation in the playbook's seed list is sized anywhere near
that.

**3. Municipal money may displace state money rather than add to it.** The $500,000 cap is
described as including municipal Affordable Housing Trust Fund dollars. If that holds, a town
that contributes trust fund cash does not increase the total. It substitutes for state money
inside the same ceiling.

> This bears directly on the government campaign. It means **the municipal ask should stay on
> the building, not on cash.** A donated or cheap building sits outside the cap. Trust fund
> cash may sit inside it. `strategy.md` already leads with the building ask, and `schedule.md`
> already concluded that getting the building at low or no cost is the plan rather than a
> saving. This finding, if confirmed, is a second independent reason for the same order.
>
> **[VERIFY BEFORE RELYING ON IT.]** This is the single most consequential Level B claim in
> this file and it is one sentence from a search summary.

**4. The bedroom count is now a live conflict.** `schedule.md` says design at six, because six
or fewer is permitted by right under N.J.S.A. 40:55D-66.1 and seven pushes the project into
conditional use and months it does not have. SNHPLP is described as funding three to four
bedroom houses. Those pull in opposite directions.

At four beds the two constraints reconcile and the money is not even reduced: four beds times
$125,000 per bed is exactly the $500,000 cap. A fifth and sixth bed add no state subsidy.

> **[ASK NJHMFA, AND ASK BEFORE SITE SELECTION.]** Does the three to four bedroom limit
> disqualify a six bedroom home outright, or does it simply cap the subsidy? The answer changes
> what building to look for, and site control is due in about 105 days.

## FINDING 2: THERE IS A LIVE COMPARABLE IN BERGEN COUNTY, APPROVED TWO MONTHS AGO

NJHMFA approved financing in **July 2026 for supportive housing in Waldwick, Bergen County.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Size | Eight bedrooms, in two two story residences |
| Location | Wyckoff Avenue, described as a predominantly residential neighbourhood |
| Total development cost | **$2.6 million** |
| NJHMFA Special Needs Housing Trust Fund | **more than $1 million** |
| Also financed by | **Bergen County HOME Investment Partnerships Program** |
| Also financed by | **Federal Home Loan Bank** |
| First residents | 2027 |

> **Level B.** Attributed to the NJHMFA press release of 2026-07-21 and to Real Estate NJ.
> Both blocked from this environment.

### What it tells you

**It names the whole stack.** Three public and quasi public sources on one Bergen County
supportive housing deal, and per `grant_history.md` Spectrum has touched none of them. That
file found no CDBG, HOME, NJHMFA, DCA, county community development, or municipal trust fund
money in any year from 2022 to 2027. Waldwick shows two of those missing sources, HOME and
NJHMFA, funding a project of the same type in the same county in the same delivery year.

**Bergen County HOME is a county ask, and the campaign is already pointed at the county.**
`README.md` says counties outrank towns and that Bergen alone covers fourteen of the eighteen
towns. Bergen County administering HOME money that just went into an eight bedroom supportive
housing project is the most specific reason yet to lead with the county.

**Federal Home Loan Bank money requires a member bank to sponsor the application.** Spectrum
has bank relationships that are already funding relationships. `grant_history.md` records
Columbia Bank and Columbia Bank Foundation at $70,000 across 2022 to 2025, Lakeland Bank at
$25,000, and Amboy Bank Foundation and Valley in 2026. A bank that has written Spectrum
cheques for HVAC and a roof is the natural sponsor for a much larger application.
**[ASK ANTONIO: which of these banks is an FHLB member, and who is the relationship contact?]**

**The eight bedrooms are split into two residences.** That is worth noticing next to Finding 1,
point 4. Two smaller residences satisfy the three to four bedroom program limit and stay under
the six person by right threshold at the same time. One six bedroom home may satisfy the second
and fail the first.

### The uncomfortable number

**$2.6 million for eight beds is $325,000 per bed.** Spectrum's budget is $1 million all in.
At the Waldwick rate that buys about three beds.

Caveats, stated honestly. Waldwick appears to be construction of two residences rather than
purchase and renovation of an existing house, it is in what NJHMFA called one of the state's
most competitive housing markets, and I have no breakdown of what the $2.6 million covers.
Acquisition and rehabilitation of an existing house should cost less per bed.

It still points one way. **The binding constraint on this project may be the budget, not the
site.** The state's own $125,000 per bed subsidy ceiling implies the state expects a project
cost well above that per bed. Nothing in this campaign has tested the $1 million against a real
Bergen County number, and `schedule.md` declined to quote one for lack of verified data.
Waldwick is the first external figure available, and it is not reassuring.

> **[ASK ANTONIO: where did the $1 million come from, and what does it assume per bed?]** If it
> was set before the high needs medical specification was fixed, it needs re-testing. A high
> needs medical home requires renovation to a licensed standard for adults needing 24 hour
> nursing, which is the most expensive renovation in the portfolio, not the cheapest.

## FINDING 5: THE FOUNDATION TIMELINE DOES NOT REACH ACQUISITION

Today is 18 September 2026.

| Milestone | Date | Days from today |
|---|---|---|
| Site control needed | ~1 January 2027 | **105** |
| Occupancy due | 31 December 2027 | **469** |

The playbook's calendar submits its first letters of inquiry in week 4, around mid October, and
full proposals in weeks 7 and 8, around December. Foundation cycles are measured in quarters.
The Hyde and Watson windows found above are illustrative: applications open 15 December to
1 February, and again 15 June to 1 August, with decisions after that.

**A foundation campaign started today cannot produce money in time to buy a building.** It can
produce money for renovation, which is the back half of the schedule, and that is a real and
worthwhile target. It cannot be on the critical path for site control.

This is the reason to run the stack in the order at the top of this file. The 105 day clock is
the scarce resource, and the first three sources are also the largest.

## IF THE FOUNDATION TRACK RUNS, RUN IT AS A MECHANISM

The playbook's own maintenance plan is "set monthly calendar reminder to re-run ProPublica
searches" with no owner named. That is a fix that decays. Three things have to be settled before
it is worth starting.

**Who owns it.** Not Antonio. Prospect research and letters of inquiry are a Development
function. **[ASK ANTONIO: who owns this, and does it run when you are unavailable?]** The
campaign already has one unassigned piece, the verification calls, per `antonio_focus.md`. It
does not need a second.

**What it costs to keep running.** The playbook's Step 2 is two to three hours of manual PDF
reading for thirty to fifty foundations, plus a monthly refresh forever. Price a commercial
prospect database against those hours before choosing the free path. Avoiding a subscription by
spending Development staff time is not automatically a saving, and this is the case where the
transaction should be automated and only the judgment kept.

**Where it lives.** One tracker, not a second parallel spreadsheet. `tracker.csv` already
exists in this folder and already has an owner column. Foundation prospects belong in the same
structure or in a clearly separate one, and not in a third place.

### Corrections to the playbook's mechanics, if it is used

- **The tools.** ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer and IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search are
  established. **FunderMatch** and **990 Scout** at philanthropy.org/990 both resolve and are
  described as free. **Grantable** resolves. **GrantCue returned nothing in search and could
  not be confirmed to exist.** Do not build a process on it without checking.
- **The Form 990-PF part numbers are not quite right.** For the 2023 form, grants and
  contributions paid are in **Part XIV, line 3**, and the application information, deadlines and
  restrictions are in **Part XIV, line 2**, the same part. The playbook sends you to Part XV for
  application information, which on the current form is Part XV-A, Analysis of Income Producing
  Activities. Part numbering has changed between form years, so read the part heading rather
  than trusting a number.
- **The worked example is not Spectrum's project.** The drafting prompt is written around a
  $2.5 million six unit acquisition with a $350,000 ask. Spectrum's project is $1 million all in
  for one home. Any letter built on the supplied prompt will misstate the project.
- **The drafting prompt invites pasting programme detail into a third party tool.** Aggregate
  and de-identified only. No resident information, no incident detail, no clinical description
  of the people who will live in the home.
