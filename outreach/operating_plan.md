# Operating Plan

A critical read of this campaign, written 2026-08-31, in the terms an experienced COO would
use. The strategy document says how to run outreach. This says whether the campaign as
designed can actually deliver the thing it exists to deliver, and where it breaks.

Short answer: the outreach is sound. The plan around it has six holes. Two are serious.

## SIX HOLES, AND THE FIX FOR EACH

### 1. The plan measures activity, not outcomes

The brief runs on phases and months. Phase 4 ends with "a one page summary of results." That
is an activity plan. It can be executed perfectly and still produce no building.

**Fix.** State the outcome as a number with a date, and make everything else subordinate to
it. Proposed, for Antonio to set:

| Outcome | Target | By |
|---|---|---|
| Sites under serious discussion | 3 | End of week 12 |
| Site under written agreement, lease or transfer | 1 | [ANTONIO TO SET, driven by the 2027 answer] |
| County funding applications submitted | 2 | Next open CDBG or HOME cycle |
| Municipal trust fund requests submitted | 3 | End of week 12 |

Three sites in discussion, not one. You need redundancy because most will die.

### 2. The whole campaign is single threaded on one blocked input

Every one of the 51 contacts reads `TO VERIFY` because no government website is reachable.
The current plan is to wait for a network change. **A COO does not let a $1 million project
sit idle waiting on IT.**

**Fix: use the phone.** Call the town's main number and ask the clerk's office for the
clerk's name and email. That is roughly 18 calls, one afternoon of work, and it unblocks the
entire campaign today.

It is arguably a **better** source than a website. Websites go stale between elections and
resignations. A person in the building telling you their own email address does not. And a
phone number is self verifying: if you dial it and the borough answers as the borough, you
have confirmed you reached the right government.

### 3. The funnel is too narrow to reliably produce one site

Rough arithmetic, using **planning assumptions, not data.** Replace these with real numbers
as soon as the first week produces any.

| Stage | Assumed rate | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Towns in Band 1 | | 18 |
| Clerks who send documents | 50 percent | 9 |
| Towns holding a usable surplus parcel | 40 percent | 4 |
| Towns willing to discuss it for a group home | 25 percent | 1 |

That lands on exactly one site with no margin. For a must have outcome, a COO wants roughly
three times the coverage. **Four ways to widen the top, in the order I would do them.**

**a. Scale the church channel. This is the strongest idea in this document.**
Spectrum already runs two Adult Training Centers in church buildings, North Haledon in a
church since 1989 and Teaneck since 2006. That is not a workaround, it is a proven model with
two live examples and decades of operating history. Churches hold underused real estate, are
mission aligned, and can decide without a council vote or a public referendum. **They are
faster than municipalities by an order of magnitude.** Spectrum also holds an Office of Faith
Based Initiatives grant, so the state relationship exists too.
Nobody has to be persuaded that this works. It already works, twice.

**b. County owned land.** Counties hold property as well as money, and one county conversation
covers many towns at once. Already in the plan as Band 5 planning departments. Move it up.

**c. Open Tier 3.** The brief called for Tier 3, towns with unmet obligations or surplus land,
and it is still empty because the data was unreachable. Spectrum operates in 14 Bergen towns.
There are considerably more in the county. [VERIFY: the exact number of Bergen County
municipalities. Not asserted here.]

**d. Name the market purchase fallback.** Spectrum owns 23 of 27 buildings, so it plainly can
acquire property. The municipal route is a way to save money and build political goodwill. It
is not the only way to get a building. Saying so out loud removes the quiet panic that makes
teams accept bad sites.

### 5. Funding cycles are the real constraint, and nobody knows the dates

CDBG and HOME run on annual application cycles. Miss one and you wait a year. On a 2027
deadline, missing a cycle is not a delay, it is a project failure.

**This makes the single most valuable unknown in the campaign a set of dates, not a set of
contacts.**

**Fix.** The first question in every county conversation, ahead of any pitch:

> When does your next CDBG and HOME application cycle open and close, and what is the
> eligibility threshold for a nonprofit subrecipient?

Get those three dates, put them at the top of the tracker, and schedule the campaign around
them. If a cycle closes inside the 90 days, that county jumps the queue regardless of its
reply probability.

### 6. The warmest assets Spectrum owns are sitting unused

The campaign is built on cold outreach while the grant file shows a network nobody has
mapped. Columbia Bank, $70,000 since 2022 and a funder since 2018, is a New Jersey community
bank whose executives sit on local boards. The Chinery Foundation committed $750,000. Valley
Hospital Foundation, the Palestroni Foundation, and the Bolger Foundation are all repeat
funders. And Spectrum was paid $248,660 to place its own clients on **Bergen County advisory
boards**, which means Spectrum people are already sitting in county rooms.

**Fix, and do it before the first cold email goes out.** Run a warm introduction audit:

1. List Spectrum's board members and their town of residence and civic roles.
2. Ask each of the top five funders for one introduction, naming the specific town or county.
3. Ask the staff supporting the advisory board placements who they deal with at the county.

One warm introduction outperforms a dozen cold emails. This is a half day of work with a
higher expected return than any week of Band 5 sending.

## WEEKLY RHYTHM

Thirty minutes, same time each week, Antonio chairs. Four numbers and one decision.

1. Touches sent this week, against 13.
2. Replies received, and the running reply rate.
3. Meetings booked.
4. Named parcels or buildings in play.
5. One decision: which town or county gets dropped this week.

That last one matters most. Campaigns fail by spreading thin across 51 contacts who are all
"still warm." Kill the dead ones early and put the time into the live ones.
