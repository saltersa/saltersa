# Follow Up Email

Sent 7 days after the call, when there has been no reply. This is touch three in the
cadence: email, call 5 business days later, this email 7 days after the call.

Keep it shorter than the first email. Give them an easy way to say "not now," because a
"not now" is worth more than silence.

---

## VARIANT A: TOWNSHIP OR BOROUGH

**Subject:** Following up, [TOWN] surplus property

Dear [TITLE] [LAST NAME],

I left you a message last week about surplus [TOWN] property. I know your calendar is full.

One question, and a single line answers it. Is this worth 20 minutes, or is now the wrong
time?

If it is the wrong time, tell me when to come back and I will.

The short version, in case it is useful. Spectrum for Living has run housing for people
with developmental and physical disabilities in New Jersey since 1986. We already operate a
group home in [TOWN]. Every person we house is low or moderate income. We are looking for a
site for a home serving adults who need nursing care 24 hours a day.

Thank you,

[SIGNATURE BLOCK]

## AFTER THIS EMAIL

If there is still no reply, stop emailing this person. Three touches is the cadence, and a
fourth costs more goodwill than it earns.

Do two things instead.

1. Log it in `tracker.csv`. Set `reply_status` to no reply, and set `next_action` to a
   revisit date roughly 90 days out.
2. Try a different door in the same jurisdiction. If the mayor went quiet, the administrator
   or the clerk may not. Log that as a new contact, not as a fourth touch on the old one.
