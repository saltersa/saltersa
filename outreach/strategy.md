# Campaign Strategy

Written 2026-08-31. Read this before sending anything.

---

## The strategy in one sentence

**Ask for information first, money second, land third**, because that is the order in which
government says yes.

Most nonprofits do the reverse. They open by asking a mayor for land, which is the largest
ask, aimed at the busiest person, with nothing behind it. That gets silence.

---

## Two things to change about how Spectrum shows up

**1. Lead with ownership, not need.** Spectrum owns 23 of its 27 buildings across 18 towns,
some for more than 40 years. That is not a charity asking for a favor. That is a long term
property owner and institutional neighbor. Towns get asked for land constantly by people who
have never built anything. Spectrum has.

**2. Sell capacity, not sympathy.** Everyone who approaches a town has a sad story. Almost
nobody can operate housing with 24 hour skilled nursing. That is the scarce thing. The pitch
is not "these people need help." It is "we can run what you cannot."

---

## How the order was decided

Targets are ranked by **probability of reply**, not by how much Spectrum wants from them.
Four things predict a reply from a New Jersey municipal or county office.

| Predictor | Why it works |
|---|---|
| The recipient has a duty to answer | A records request is not a proposal. There is nothing to evaluate and no reason to refuse. |
| Answering is literally their job | A CDBG officer exists to move money to eligible groups. Replying is the work, not an interruption. |
| A documented prior relationship exists | Spectrum has been paid by this county before. That converts a cold email into a follow up. |
| Small, concrete, cheap to grant | "Send me the list" costs a minute. "Give us land" costs a council vote. |

Elected officials score badly on all four. That is why mayors come late, not because they do
not matter.

## The five bands, highest chance of reply first

### Band 1. Municipal clerks and administrators, records request. 18 contacts.

**Chance of reply: very high.** This is not a pitch, so there is nothing to say no to.
Processing records requests is routine clerk work.

**Ask:** the housing element and fair share plan, the adopted affordable housing trust fund
spending plan, and the list of municipally owned property.

**Why this is the right opening move.** It produces the exact facts the campaign is missing.
Right now Variant A of the cold email contains a bracket that says "verify this town's
obligation before sending." Band 1 fills that bracket. Every later email stops being a guess.

It also sorts the 18 towns into responsive and unresponsive **before** Spectrum spends a
mayor level ask on any of them.

> citing a deadline to a clerk. The strategy does not depend on it. It only gets stronger.

### Band 2. County community development offices. 3 contacts.

**Chance of reply: high.** Their job is to place CDBG and HOME money with eligible
subrecipients. Spectrum is textbook eligible, because 100 percent of the people it houses are
low or moderate income and Medicaid funded.

**This is also the highest leverage band in the whole campaign.** Bergen County alone covers
14 of the 18 towns and 19 of the 27 facilities. One good conversation there is worth more
than fourteen separate town emails.

Order within the band, and why:

1. **Bergen County.** Spectrum was paid $248,660 through Inclusive Healthy Communities to
   place the people it serves onto **Bergen County advisory boards**. A funded working
   relationship with this exact county government already exists. Open with that sentence.
   It is the single warmest door in the campaign.
2. **Middlesex County.** The Arts Institute of Middlesex County has funded Spectrum in five
   consecutive years, $30,775.32 total. A county affiliated tie, weaker than Bergen's but
   real. Spectrum's largest single town cluster, four facilities, is in Edison.
3. **Passaic County.** No documented relationship and the thinnest footprint, three towns and
   four facilities. Goes last for that reason.

### Band 4. The two Adult Training Center rental asks. 2 contacts.

**Chance of reply: medium high, and the ask is the most concrete in the campaign.**

Teaneck and North Haledon both host an Adult Training Center that Spectrum does not own. Both
are owned by churches. This is not a hypothetical request for land at some future date. It is
a specific operational need with a building attached.

**Ask:** would the town rent Spectrum underused municipal space.

No rent figure appears, per the ruling of 2026-08-31.

### Band 5. Mayors, county executives, human services, planning, housing authorities.

**Chance of reply: low. Value if they engage: highest.**

Send here only after Band 1 or Band 3 produced something. A mayor contacted cold about land
is the lowest yield email in the campaign. A mayor contacted with "your administrator sent me
your surplus property list and one parcel looks right" is a different email entirely.

Salvador Moran signs everything in this band.

## Four ideas worth using

**1. The open house is the strongest tool Spectrum has.** Phase 4 already calls for inviting
officials to a group home. Use the **Closter intermediate care facility**: 60 beds, 29,500
square feet, 24 hour skilled nursing, operating since 1983. An official who walks through it
understands in ten minutes what no email can convey. Schedule it early and invite every Band
2 and Band 5 contact who replied at all.

**2. Bundle the three asks in one meeting.** Land, funding, and partnership sit in different
pots controlled by different people. A county can fund a project sitting on municipal land.
The 20 minute agenda already closes on all three. Do not split them across three meetings.

**3. Make the "no" easy.** The follow up email asks "is this worth 20 minutes, or is now the
wrong time." A fast no is worth more than silence, because it frees the slot and often comes
with a referral to the right person.

**4. Ask every single contact one extra question.** "Who else should I be talking to?" A name
handed over by someone inside the building outperforms any cold list. Log every referral in
`tracker.csv` as a new contact, and verify it against the official site before writing.

## What would sink this campaign

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Writing to a guessed name or email | All 51 contacts stay `TO VERIFY` until an official page is read. No exceptions. |
| Claiming a Spectrum home earns a town affordable housing credit | Not stated anywhere. Needs a legal source first. It is the strongest line available and it is still not worth fabricating. |
| Asserting a town's obligation or trust fund balance | Bracketed in Variant A. Band 1 exists to replace the bracket with fact. |
| Naming a rent figure | Prohibited in outreach as of 2026-08-31. |
| Contacting Westwood before the lease conflict is settled | Both Westwood rows frozen in `targets.csv`. |
| Burning a mayor on a cold land ask | Mayors are Band 5, after evidence exists. |
