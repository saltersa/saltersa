# Part 2. General Information

## 1. Purpose

Spectrum for Living supports about 1,000 people across 14 group homes, 5 supervised apartments, 5 adult day programs, and an intermediate care facility in Closter. Homes operate 24 hours a day. When a computer, a phone, a boiler, or a wheelchair lift stops working, care, documentation, billing, and safety are all affected at once.

This manual does four things:

1. Gives every employee one clear way to report a problem and get it fixed.
2. Protects the private information of the people we support, our employees, our donors, and our vendors.
3. Keeps homes, day programs, offices, and vehicles running when something fails.
4. Gives leadership a regular set of numbers so problems are visible before they become emergencies.

## 2. Scope

This manual applies to every person who uses a Spectrum for Living account, device, building, or vehicle. That includes employees, Direct Support Professionals, nurses, supervisors, managers, directors, executives, per diem and temporary staff, interns, volunteers, contractors, vendors, and board members.

It covers all locations in Bergen, Passaic, and Middlesex counties, all offices, all homes and day programs, the Closter ICF, maintenance and fleet locations, and any place where staff work remotely.

Some subjects belong to other departments and are not covered here. Employee relations matters go to Human Resources. Incidents involving a person we support follow the Unusual Incident Report process. Clinical questions follow the clinical chain of command. Life safety emergencies go to 911.

## 3. Who Does What

## 4. How the Ticket System Works

Both Google Forms feed a Google Sheet that Spectrum for Living owns. That sheet is the agency's record.

1. A staff member submits a form.
2. The response lands in the Spectrum owned sheet and an email alert goes to the responder, either Aspen Technology or Facilities.
3. The responder updates the status column in the sheet as work progresses.
4. The Technology Coordinator reviews the technology sheet every business day. The Director of Facilities and Fleet Management reviews the property sheet every business day.
5. Nothing is closed until the person who submitted it confirms the problem is fixed.
6. The Chief Operating Officer pulls the monthly numbers from the same sheet.

**Why the sheet matters.** If tickets live only inside a vendor's system, Spectrum has no record of what broke, how often, or how long it took, and no way to hold anyone to a response time. Keeping the sheet in agency hands is what makes the monthly report possible and what protects the agency if the vendor ever changes.

## 6. Requesting an Exception

An exception is written permission to do something differently for a set period. Doing something differently without written permission is a violation.

1. Complete Form I.
2. State the rule you cannot follow and the operating reason.
3. Describe what you will do instead to reduce the risk.
4. Send it to the Chief Operating Officer.

| Risk level | Approved by | Maximum length |
|---|---|---|
| Low, such as a non standard accessory | Chief Operating Officer | 12 months |
| Moderate, such as delaying a computer replacement | Chief Operating Officer | 6 months |
| High, involving 2-Step Verification, encryption, backups, health information, payments, or life safety equipment | Chief Operating Officer and VP Compliance | 90 days |
| Critical, affecting HIPAA, CMS, DDD, licensing, or insurance requirements | Chief Executive Officer and legal counsel | 30 days |

**Never approved.** Sharing a password. Turning off 2-Step Verification on an account that reaches email, health information, finance, payroll, or Human Resources systems. Resetting a password from an email request alone. Changing vendor banking information without a verification call. Entering information about a person we support into an AI tool. Keeping confidential information on a device that is not encrypted. Disabling, blocking, or bypassing fire and life safety equipment.

## 8. Enforcement

Staff will not be disciplined for promptly reporting a mistake they made. Hiding a mistake is the serious problem, not making one.

| Situation | Typical response |
|---|---|
| Honest mistake, reported right away | Coaching and refresher training |
| Repeating the same mistake after coaching | Documented counseling by the supervisor |
| Looking at records without a work reason | Investigation by Human Resources and Compliance, possible termination |
| Sharing a password or working around a security control | Access review and documented discipline |
| Bypassing or disabling life safety equipment | Immediate investigation and discipline |
| Hiding an incident | Discipline up to termination |
| Theft, fraud, or deliberate damage | Termination and referral to law enforcement |

Contractors and vendors lose access immediately and the contract is reviewed by the Director of Finance. Volunteers and interns lose access and the sponsoring supervisor is notified. Board member matters go to the Board Chair.

## TEC-01. Submitting a Technology Ticket

| Use this when | Anything with a computer, tablet, email, password, account, printer, desk phone, internet, software, or agency cell phone |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Technology Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Goes to | Aspen Technology |
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer |

**Before you submit**

1. Make sure it is not an emergency. If anyone is in danger, call 911 first, then the program on call number.
2. Try the short checks in the procedure for your problem. TEC-02 for computers, TEC-03 for printers, TEC-04 for phones, TEC-05 for internet.
3. Take a photo of any error message or of the equipment.

**Filling out the form**

1. Give your name, job title, and a phone number that will actually be answered. A desk phone that is broken is not a contact number.
2. Choose your site from the list, then say the exact room, office, or unit.
3. Enter the asset tag, serial number, or extension number if there is one. The asset tag is the small label with a number on it.
4. Type the error message exactly as it appears on the screen.
5. Say when the problem started and whether it has happened before.
6. Say who is affected and what work cannot be done.
7. Answer whether services to people we support are affected.
8. Attach the photo.
9. Choose the priority using the table in Part 1.
10. Give the best time for someone to visit and any access instructions, such as a locked room or a doorbell that does not work.
11. Submit, and write down the ticket number.

**If the priority is emergency or urgent, also call Aspen Technology at [insert number] and give the ticket number.** Do not rely on the form alone when a site cannot operate.

**Do not**

1. Do not send a text message or an email to an individual instead of submitting the form. Requests handled that way leave no record, and no one can pick them up when that person is off.
2. Do not submit the same problem twice. If you need to add information, reply to the ticket confirmation.
3. Do not wait until the end of your shift to report something that is stopping work now.

**What happens next**

1. You receive a confirmation with your ticket number.
2. Aspen Technology responds within the time for the priority you chose.
3. You receive updates. Emergency tickets are updated every 30 minutes.
4. The ticket is not closed until you confirm the problem is fixed.
5. If the same problem happens three times in 90 days, it is flagged for a repair or replace decision.

**If nothing happens.** No response to an emergency ticket in 15 minutes, call [insert Aspen number]. Still nothing in 30 minutes, call the Technology Coordinator at [insert number]. Still nothing in one hour, call the Chief Operating Officer.

**Records.** Tickets are kept 7 years in the agency ticket sheet.

## TEC-02. Computer Will Not Start or Is Not Working

| Use this when | A desktop, laptop, or tablet will not start, freezes, is very slow, or will not connect |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Technology Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer |

**Try this first**

1. Check that the power cable is pushed in at both the computer and the wall, and that the outlet works. Test the outlet with a lamp or phone charger. **If the outlet has no power, submit a Property Ticket instead.**
2. For a laptop, plug in the charger and wait five minutes before trying again. A fully drained battery will not respond right away.
3. Press and hold the power button for 10 seconds to shut it down, then press it once to start it again.
4. Check that the monitor is on and that its cable is pushed in at both ends.
5. Write down any message on the screen exactly as it appears, and take a photo.
6. If the computer starts but is very slow, close every program, restart it, and wait five minutes before judging.
7. If it will not connect to the network, check whether other devices at the site are working. If they are not, this is an internet problem. Go to TEC-05.

**Do not**

1. Do not open the computer or remove any parts.
2. Do not install software to try to fix it.
3. Do not move a desktop computer to another room. The network jack it uses may not work elsewhere.
4. Do not keep working on a computer that is behaving strangely if you think it may have a virus. Go to TEC-09.

**Call instead of submitting a form if**

1. The computer is the only one at the site and staff cannot document care.
2. You smell burning, see smoke, or the computer is hot to the touch. Unplug it if it is safe and call.
3. You think the computer has a virus or ransomware.

**What happens next**

1. Aspen Technology will contact you, and may connect remotely.
2. If the computer cannot be fixed the same day and the site has no other computer, ask for a loaner in the ticket.
3. Before any computer is replaced, your files must be moved. Tell Aspen Technology where your files are saved.

**A note on saving files.** Save your work in Google Drive, not on the computer itself. Files saved only on a computer are not backed up and are lost when that computer fails.

**Records.** Ticket history by device is kept for the life of the device plus 7 years.

## TEC-03. Printer Problems

| Use this when | A printer or copier will not print, jams, or shows an error |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Technology Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer |

**Try this first**

1. Read the message on the printer screen. Write it down and take a photo.
2. If the screen is dark, check the power switch and both ends of the power cord.
3. Open the paper tray. Check that the paper is squared, below the fill line, and not damp. Fan the stack, reload it, and close the tray until it clicks.
4. Look for a toner message. Replace the toner only if you have been trained and a spare is on site.
5. Check that the network cable is pushed in at the printer and at the wall. Do not change any settings.
6. Open the print queue on your computer, cancel any stuck jobs, and print one test page.
7. Check that you selected the right printer. Printing to the wrong device is the most common report of a broken printer.
8. Turn the printer off, wait 30 seconds, turn it on, and wait up to three minutes for it to finish starting.
9. If the problem is only on your computer, restart your computer.

**Paper jams.** Read the screen for the location. Open only the doors the screen or the diagram shows. Pull the paper slowly in the direction it was traveling, never backward. Remove every piece, including small torn corners. Close all doors firmly. If the jam message returns after two tries, stop and submit a ticket.

**Do not**

1. Do not open internal panels or reach past the paper and toner areas. Parts inside are hot enough to burn.
2. Do not use tools or force to remove paper.
3. Do not attempt repairs on leased equipment.
4. Do not install printer software from the internet.
5. Do not move a printer. Submit a ticket instead.
6. Do not connect a personal printer to an agency computer.

**Printing private information**

1. Print only to a printer you can reach right away. Never print confidential material to another floor or another site.
2. Stand at the printer when printing anything with health, personnel, or financial information.
3. Collect your pages immediately. Do not leave them to pick up later.
4. If you find someone else's confidential pages, give them to the owner or your supervisor, or put them in the locked shredding bin.
5. Shred confidential paper in a locked container, never in trash or recycling.
6. If confidential pages printed somewhere you cannot reach them, tell your supervisor, call Aspen Technology, and report it to Compliance within one hour.

**Call right away** if there is smoke, a burning smell, sparking, or an electrical shock. Unplug the printer if it is safe, and call 911 if there is smoke or fire.

**Supplies.** Each site keeps one spare toner for each installed cartridge. Reorder when you take the last spare, not when you run out. Order through [insert process].

**Records.** Ticket history and service cost by printer are kept for the life of the device plus 7 years.

## TEC-04. Desk Phone Problems

| Use this when | A desk phone has no dial tone, will not ring, or sounds bad |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Technology Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer |

**Safety first.** Every site must have a working way to call 911 at all times. If the phones at a site go down, the supervisor tells everyone on shift what the backup method is, confirms each staff member has a working cell phone or the site backup phone, calls the program on call number, and writes down the time.

Agency phones send the address they are registered to when someone dials 911. **A phone moved to another room or site sends the wrong address.** Never move a phone yourself. Submit a ticket and Aspen Technology will update the registered address first.

**Try this first**

1. Look at the phone screen. Is it lit? What does it say? Take a photo.
2. If the screen is dark, check that the network cable is pushed in at the phone and at the wall jack. Most phones get their power through that cable.
3. Do not swap the two cables on the back of the phone. One feeds the phone and one feeds the computer.
4. Check whether other phones at the site have the same problem. If several do, report it as one ticket for the site.
5. Check whether the internet is working. If the internet is down, the phones will be down too. Report it once, not twice.
6. To restart the phone, unplug the network cable, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in, and wait up to three minutes. Then call another extension to test it.

**Common problems**

| Problem | What to try |
|---|---|
| No dial tone | Check the handset cord at both ends, then try the speakerphone button. If the speaker works, the handset or its cord is the problem. |
| No incoming calls | Look for a Do Not Disturb or forwarding symbol on the screen and turn it off. Ask a coworker to call your extension and your direct number. |
| No outgoing calls | Write down the exact error or recording. Try an internal extension, then a local number, then long distance, and note which fail. |
| Choppy or echoing sound | Test the handset, the speaker, and the headset. Note the time of day and whether it happens on every call. |
| Voicemail full | Delete old messages, but keep any message that records a service or an incident. |
| Forgot the voicemail PIN | Submit a ticket. Never share a PIN with a coworker. |
| Headset not working | Check that it is in the headset port, not the handset port, and press the headset button. |

**Voicemail greeting.** Give your name, title, and agency, name someone else to contact, and end with "If this is an emergency, please hang up and dial 911."

**Changes that need a ticket.** New extensions, reassigned extensions, call groups, call forwarding, the main number menu, voicemail PIN resets, access to a former employee's voicemail, and moving a phone. Staff do not contact the phone company directly.

Forwarding a home's main line or an on call line to a personal cell phone needs written approval from the VP of Operations and the Chief Operating Officer.

**Call instead of submitting a form** if a site has no working phone at all.

**Records.** Outage log with start and end times, the backup method used, and the registered address check for every phone.

## TEC-09. Cybersecurity Rules for Staff

| Use this when | Any time something looks wrong with a message, a computer, or an account |
|---|---|
| Contact | Call Aspen Technology at [insert number] |
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer |

**If something looks wrong: stop, disconnect, report.** Stop typing and do not click anything else. Unplug the network cable and turn off the Wi-Fi. **Do not turn the computer off** unless there is smoke, fire, or a safety hazard, because turning it off destroys information needed to find out what happened. Call Aspen Technology at [insert number], or [insert emergency number] after hours, then tell your supervisor. **You will not be disciplined for reporting a mistake promptly. Not reporting is the serious problem.**

**Ten rules**

1. Slow down when a message says it is urgent. Urgency is the main tool attackers use.
2. Verify any request about money or personal information by phone, using a number you already have, not a number in the message.
3. Never share your password. No one will ever ask you for it.
4. Never approve a 2-Step Verification prompt you did not start. If prompts keep coming, someone has your password. Deny them and call.
5. Hover over a link before you click. If the address does not match the text, do not click.
6. Do not open an attachment you were not expecting. Call the sender first.
7. Lock your screen every time you walk away.
8. Install updates when prompted.
9. Report anything suspicious within one hour.
10. When you are not sure, ask. A phone call costs nothing.

**What to watch for**

| Threat | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Phishing | A message that creates urgency, asks you to sign in, or asks for money or information |
| Executive impersonation | A message that looks like it came from the CEO or COO, often from an outside address, asking for gift cards, a wire, or employee data |
| Vendor fraud | A vendor writes that their bank account has changed |
| Virus or ransomware | Sudden slowness, programs starting on their own, security software turned off, files renamed or unreadable, a note demanding payment |
| Social engineering | A caller claiming to be from Aspen Technology, a vendor, or a state agency, asking you to install something or let them in |

**No executive at Spectrum for Living will ever ask you by email or text to buy gift cards, send a wire transfer, or send employee payroll information. Any such message is fraud.**

**If you get a suspicious email.** Do not click, reply, open attachments, or forward it to coworkers. Report it with the Gmail three dot menu, Report phishing, and forward it to [insert address]. If it involves money or looks like it came from an executive, also call Aspen Technology and tell the Director of Finance. Full steps are on QR-2.

**If you already clicked or typed your password.** Call Aspen Technology immediately. Do not send an email. Disconnect from the network. Do not change your own password first, because they will reset it and sign the attacker out at the same time. Tell your supervisor.

**If you think a computer has a virus.** Stop using it, disconnect it, do not turn it off, photograph the screen, and call. Tell coworkers not to use shared drives until you are told it is safe. Full steps are on QR-3.

**Lost or stolen device and disposal.** Call immediately, day or night, and tell your supervisor. File a police report if it was stolen. The device will be locked and erased remotely. Shred confidential paper in a locked container, and never throw away, sell, or donate an agency device yourself.

## TEC-10. Reporting a Security or Privacy Incident

| Use this when | Private information may have been seen, sent, or taken by the wrong person |
|---|---|
| Contact | Call Aspen Technology at [insert number], then complete Form E |
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer, with VP Compliance |

**What to report.** Any suspected or actual exposure of information about a person we support, an employee, a donor, or agency finances. This includes email sent to the wrong person, a lost document or device, a file shared with the wrong people, an unlocked screen seen by a visitor, a suspected virus, a suspected account break in, a payment fraud attempt, and anyone looking at records without a work reason.

**How to report.** Call within one hour of noticing. Tell your supervisor and complete Form E the same day. Do not delete anything, because the emails, files, and device are the record of what happened. Do not discuss the event outside the agency or on social media.

**Levels**

| Level | Description | Who is told |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Critical | Confirmed breach, active ransomware, agency wide outage, money sent to a fraudster, health information exposed | CEO, COO, Compliance, Finance, legal counsel, insurance carrier, Board Chair |
| 2. High | Confirmed account break in, stolen device with health information, health information sent outside the agency | COO, Compliance, and Finance if money is involved |
| 3. Moderate | Contained, limited exposure, such as a phishing click with no password entered | COO, and Compliance if health information is involved |
| 4. Low | No exposure, such as a suspicious message reported and not clicked | Technology Coordinator |

The Chief Operating Officer assigns the level, with advice from Aspen Technology. Anyone may raise it. When in doubt, start higher.

**What Aspen Technology does.** Confirms the report within 30 minutes. Saves the evidence before changing anything. Contains the problem by isolating devices, disabling accounts, and signing out active sessions. Determines what information and how many people are affected. Removes the cause, resets affected passwords, and restores from a clean backup if needed. Records every step in the ticket.

**Who decides what**

1. The VP Compliance decides whether the event is a reportable breach under HIPAA or New Jersey law. Aspen Technology does not make that decision, and neither does the person who reported it.
2. The Director of Finance notifies the cyber insurance carrier. Most policies require notice within a set number of hours and require the use of the carrier's approved vendors. Calling late can void coverage.
3. The Chief Executive Officer and legal counsel handle any contact with law enforcement, the media, or a regulator.
4. No one pays a ransom or communicates with an attacker without the Chief Executive Officer, legal counsel, and the insurance carrier.

A security incident does not replace an Unusual Incident Report, and a UIR does not replace this report. If an event involves both, file both.

**Review after the event.** For level 1 and level 2, the Chief Operating Officer holds a review within ten business days covering what happened, how it was found, what worked, what was slow, what would have prevented it, and what will change. The review examines the process, not the individual.

**Records.** Incident reports are kept 7 years, and at least 6 years from resolution when health information is involved.

## TEC-11. Verifying Payment and Banking Changes

| Use this when | Anyone asks to change how, where, or to whom money is sent |
|---|---|
| Owner | Director of Finance |

**The rule.** No change to how, where, or to whom money is sent is ever made from an email, letter, fax, text message, or incoming phone call. Verification is always an outgoing call, by voice, to a number already in the vendor file, to a person whose name we already have.

**When this applies.** Vendor bank account or remittance changes, new vendor setup, wire transfers, ACH changes, employee direct deposit changes, payroll data requests, gift card requests, unusual or duplicate invoices, changes to a payment address, and any request to skip normal approval.

**Steps**

1. Stop. Do not process the request and do not forward it to accounting for action.
2. Complete Form H.
3. Find the vendor's phone number in the existing vendor file, the signed contract, or an invoice that predates this request. **Never use a number from the request itself, its signature block, or an attachment.**

5. Confirm the exact new account and routing numbers, the effective date, and who at the vendor authorized the change.

2. A request to keep it confidential.

Call the cyber insurance carrier before taking other steps. Report it at ic3.gov and to local police. Keep every email. Tell legal counsel, and follow TEC-10 for the mailboxes involved.

**Records.** Form H and supporting documents are kept 7 years. Finance audits a sample of banking changes each quarter.

## TEC-12. Artificial Intelligence Use

| Use this when | You want to use any AI tool for agency work |
|---|---|
| Owner | Chief Operating Officer |

**The rule that governs everything else.** AI may help you draft, summarize, and organize. It may never decide anything about a person. No private information about a real person goes into it.

**You may use an approved AI tool to** draft and improve emails, memos, and reports; outline plans, checklists, and templates; summarize notes you took yourself; draft job descriptions, contractor scopes of work, and facilities procedures; draft grant narratives; rewrite material in plain language; and summarize published regulations, as long as you check every citation against the actual rule.

**Never enter any of this into an AI tool**

1. Anything about a person we support. No names, no initials, no dates, no site names, no descriptions, no diagnoses, no incidents.
2. Any description detailed enough to identify a person. In a small service area, "the man at our Closter facility who uses a wheelchair and was recently hospitalized" identifies someone even with no name attached.
3. Employee personnel information, including reviews, discipline, medical information, and pay.
4. Social Security numbers, dates of birth, or license numbers.
5. Bank account numbers, routing numbers, or credit card information.
6. Passwords or security settings.
7. Donor information, unpublished financial statements, board materials, or legal strategy.

**Never use AI to decide**

1. Anything clinical, including assessment, diagnosis, treatment, medication, or behavior support.
2. Hiring, discipline, termination, promotion, or pay.
3. Eligibility, benefits, or service authorization for a person.
4. Housing placement, transfer, roommate matching, or discharge.
5. Anything about a person's rights, restrictions, or guardianship.
6. The final content of a regulatory submission, a Unusual Incident Report, or a survey response.

**Also not permitted.** Using a personal AI account for agency work. Using a tool that is not on the approved list at [insert location]. Turning on an AI feature inside software the agency already uses, including meeting transcription and email summarizing, without approval. Recording or transcribing a meeting with an AI tool without the consent of everyone present. Publishing anything an AI tool produced without checking it.

**Before you type, check five things**

1. Is this tool on the approved list?
2. Am I signed in with my Spectrum for Living account?
3. Could anything I am entering identify a real person?
4. Is this a decision about a person? If yes, stop.
5. Can I check every fact it gives back?

**After it answers.** Open every citation, because AI invents regulation numbers, statistics, and quotations that look real. Check every number against its actual source. Read for language that is dismissive of people with disabilities or that takes away a person's role in their own life. **You are responsible for what you send. "The AI wrote it" is not an explanation.**

**Report a problem.** Submit a ticket if an AI tool produced something wrong that was nearly used, or produced biased or demeaning language. If personal information was entered by mistake, call Aspen Technology right away. No one is disciplined for reporting an AI mistake in good faith.

This procedure requires review by Human Resources, Compliance, Clinical, and legal counsel before adoption.

# Part 4. Property Procedures

Property tickets go to Facilities and Fleet Management through the Property Ticket Form. This part covers buildings, building systems, grounds, and vehicles. It does not cover computers, phones, or networks. Those are in Part 3.

**Homes are people's homes.** A repair visit is a visit to someone's residence. Knock, introduce yourself, explain what you are there to do, and work around the routines of the people who live there whenever you can.

## PRO-01. Submitting a Property Ticket

| Use this when | Anything about a building, grounds, appliance, furniture, vehicle, door, lock, or accessibility equipment |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Goes to | Facilities and Fleet Management |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Before you submit**

1. Make sure it is not an emergency. If anyone is in danger, if you smell gas, if there is smoke or fire, or if water is near electricity, call **911** first, then the program on call number.
2. Make the area safe if you can do so without risk. Put out a cone or a chair, close a door, or keep people away from the area.
3. Take a photo.

**Filling out the form**

1. Give your name, job title, and a phone number that will be answered.
2. Choose your site, then say the exact room or area. "Second floor bathroom, left side" is useful. "Upstairs" is not.
3. Say what is wrong in plain words. You do not need to know the cause or the name of the part.
4. Say when it started and whether it has happened before.
5. Say how it affects the people who live or attend there. This drives the priority more than anything else.
6. Answer whether anyone's safety, health, or daily routine is affected.
7. Attach the photo.
8. Choose the priority using the table in Part 1.
9. Give the best times to visit, and note anything a visitor needs to know: a resident who is uncomfortable with strangers, a nap schedule, a dog in the yard, a door that sticks, where to park.
10. Submit, and write down the ticket number.

**If the priority is emergency or urgent, also call Facilities at [insert number] and give the ticket number.**

**What makes a good ticket.** Weak: "It is cold." Strong: "Home 3, the whole first floor is cold. Thermostat reads 58 and is set to 70. The boiler is making a clicking noise and the reset button is red. Four people live here, two use wheelchairs and cannot move around to stay warm. It is 24 degrees outside."

**Do not**

1. Do not attempt a repair you have not been trained and authorized to do. This includes anything electrical, anything involving gas, anything on a ladder above shoulder height, and anything requiring a tool you do not normally use.
2. Do not call a contractor yourself. All contractors are arranged by Facilities.
3. Do not buy parts or supplies and seek reimbursement. Order through Facilities.
4. Do not text or email a maintenance staff member directly. Requests handled that way leave no record and stall when that person is off.

**What happens next**

1. You receive a confirmation with your ticket number.
2. Facilities responds within the time for the priority.
3. You receive updates, and emergency tickets are updated every 30 minutes.
4. The ticket is not closed until the site confirms the work is finished and the area is clean.
5. If the same problem happens three times in 90 days, it is flagged for a repair or replace decision.

**If nothing happens.** No response to an emergency ticket in 15 minutes, call Facilities at [insert number]. Still nothing in 30 minutes, call the Director of Facilities and Fleet Management. Still nothing in one hour, call the Chief Operating Officer.

**Records.** Tickets are kept 7 years, with the work done, parts, labor, and vendor cost.

## PRO-02. Heating, Cooling, and Hot Water

| Use this when | A site is too cold or too hot, or there is no hot water |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Treat this as an emergency at any home when** there is no heat and the outside temperature is below [insert temperature], there is no cooling and the indoor temperature is above [insert temperature], or anyone at the site has a medical condition affected by temperature. **Call Facilities immediately.** Licensing requires that homes maintain a safe temperature, and a temperature failure at a home is a resident health issue, not a comfort issue.

**Check these before you submit**

1. Read the thermostat. Write down what it reads and what it is set to.
2. Confirm the thermostat is set to heat or cool, not to off or fan.
3. Check that the thermostat is not in a program or hold mode someone set by mistake.
4. Check whether the problem is the whole building or one room. One cold room usually means a vent, a radiator, or a window.
5. Check that vents and radiators are not blocked by furniture.
6. For no hot water, check whether it is one faucet or the whole house, and whether cold water works normally.
7. Take a photo of the thermostat and of any equipment showing a warning light or a red reset button.

**Do not**

1. Do not press a reset button on a boiler or furnace more than once. Repeated resets can cause a fuel buildup.
2. Do not adjust a water heater temperature setting. Scald risk in a home is a licensing matter and the setting is controlled by Facilities.
3. Do not use an unapproved space heater. Space heaters are a leading cause of residential fires and most are not permitted. If a site needs temporary heat, Facilities provides it.
4. Do not open windows in winter to fix an overheating problem before reporting it. Report it.
5. Do not block or close vents to redirect heat.

**If you smell gas, leave immediately, take everyone with you, do not touch light switches or phones inside, and call 911 from outside. Then call the gas company at [insert number] and Facilities.**

**While you wait**

1. Move people to the most comfortable part of the building.
2. Provide blankets or fluids as appropriate, following program guidance.
3. Record indoor temperatures every hour and write them in the log.
4. Tell the program on call number if anyone's health may be affected.
5. If the site cannot be kept safe, the relocation decision belongs to Operations and Clinical.

**Records.** Temperature logs during an outage, the ticket, and the repair record are kept 7 years. Repeat heating failures at the same home are reported to the Chief Operating Officer.

## PRO-03. Water Leaks, Plumbing, and Flooding

| Use this when | Water is leaking, a drain is blocked, a toilet is not working, or there is standing water |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Call 911 first if water is near electrical outlets, panels, or equipment, or if anyone could be shocked.** Do not walk into standing water that may be in contact with electricity.

**Stop the water if you safely can**

1. Find the shutoff valve. Toilets and sinks have one behind or below them. Turn it clockwise, gently. Do not force a stiff valve, because an old valve can break and make the leak much worse.
2. If you cannot find or turn the valve, call Facilities immediately.
3. Every site should have its main water shutoff location posted. If yours is not marked, submit a ticket to have it labeled.

**Then**

1. Put down towels or a bucket and keep people away from the area.
2. Photograph the leak and any water damage.
3. Report it. Actively running water you cannot stop is an emergency. A slow drip is routine but still gets a ticket, because a slow drip becomes a ceiling collapse.
4. Note whether the water is clean, gray, or sewage. Sewage backup is an emergency and the area must be closed off until it is cleaned properly.

**Do not**

1. Do not use chemical drain cleaners. They damage older pipes and injure whoever opens the drain next.
2. Do not plunge a toilet that is already overflowing. Turn off the valve first.
3. Do not remove a ceiling tile that is holding water. Call Facilities.
4. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
5. Do not paint over or hide a water stain. A stain is evidence of an active or past leak and is something a surveyor will ask about.

**Report a water stain, a musty smell, or peeling paint even if there is no active leak.** Those are the early signs of a hidden leak, and catching one early is the difference between a plumbing repair and a mold remediation.

**After a leak**

1. Facilities documents the source, the repair, and the drying process.
2. Any wet building material that cannot be dried within [insert number] hours is removed, not dried in place.
3. If a resident's belongings were damaged, tell the program supervisor so it is handled through program policy.

**Records.** Ticket, photographs, repair record, and drying documentation are kept 7 years. Water damage records may be needed for insurance and for licensing.

## PRO-04. Electrical Problems and Loss of Power

| Use this when | An outlet, light, or circuit is not working, or the site loses power |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Call 911 immediately** for smoke, sparks, a burning smell, an exposed wire, or anyone who has received a shock. Do not touch the equipment.

**One outlet or one light is not working**

1. Check whether anything else on that wall works.
2. Look for a reset button on the outlet itself. Outlets in bathrooms, kitchens, and outdoors have one. Press it once.
3. Check the breaker panel. Look for a breaker that is between on and off. Push it fully off, then fully on. **Do this only once.** If it trips again, stop and submit a ticket, because a breaker that keeps tripping is protecting you from a real fault.
4. Note what was plugged in when it stopped working.
5. Submit the ticket with the room and what is affected.

**The whole site loses power**

1. Account for everyone. **Check immediately on anyone who depends on powered medical or assistive equipment**, including oxygen, feeding pumps, ceiling lifts, and powered beds.
2. Call the program on call number and Facilities.
3. Check whether the neighbors have power. If the outage is on the street, report it to the electric company at [insert number] and get an estimated restoration time.
4. If the site has a generator, confirm it started and is carrying the expected load. If it did not, call Facilities immediately.
5. Write down the time the power went out.
6. Check refrigerators and freezers holding food or medication, and record temperatures. Keep the doors closed.
7. Remember that phones, Wi-Fi, and door systems stop working when the power is out, even if laptops still run on battery. Use cell phones.
8. Follow CON-01 for documentation.
9. If temperature, medical equipment, or life safety cannot be maintained, the relocation decision belongs to Operations and Clinical.

**Do not**

1. Do not use candles. Use flashlights.
2. Do not run a generator, grill, or any fuel burning device indoors or in a garage.
3. Do not open the breaker panel cover or work inside it.
4. Do not use extension cords or power strips as permanent wiring, and never daisy chain them.
5. Do not overload an outlet with high draw appliances such as heaters or microwaves.

**After power returns.** Check that the heating and cooling, refrigerators, phones, internet, door locks, alarms, and any medical equipment all came back. Record refrigerator and freezer temperatures and follow program guidance on food and medication safety. Write down the restoration time.

**Records.** Outage log, temperature records, and generator performance are kept 7 years.

## PRO-05. Appliances and Kitchen Equipment

| Use this when | A refrigerator, freezer, stove, oven, dishwasher, washer, or dryer is not working |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |

## PRO-07. Fire and Life Safety Equipment

| Use this when | Anything to do with fire alarms, smoke detectors, sprinklers, extinguishers, exit lights, emergency call systems, or evacuation equipment |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link], marked emergency |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Every report in this procedure is an emergency ticket and a phone call.** Life safety equipment protects people who may not be able to evacuate on their own. Nothing in this category waits for the next business day.

**Report immediately**

1. A smoke or carbon monoxide detector that is chirping, silent when tested, missing, or hanging loose.
2. A fire alarm panel showing trouble, fault, or supervisory.
3. A sprinkler head that is damaged, painted, leaking, or blocked by stored items.
4. A fire extinguisher that is missing, discharged, damaged, or past its inspection date.
5. An exit light or emergency light that is out.
6. An exit door or path that is blocked, or an exit sign that is not visible.
7. An emergency call system or pull cord that does not work.
8. Evacuation equipment that is missing or damaged.
9. A fire door that will not close and latch on its own.

**Never do any of these**

1. Never disable, silence, cover, or remove a detector, including to stop cooking smoke. **Report it instead.** Removing a battery from a detector in a home where people cannot self evacuate is a serious matter and is treated as such.
2. Never block an exit, a corridor, or an electrical panel with storage.
3. Never prop or wedge a fire door open.
4. Never hang anything from a sprinkler head or pipe.
5. Never store anything within 18 inches of a sprinkler head.
6. Never discharge an extinguisher for practice.

**If an alarm sounds.** Follow the site fire plan. Evacuate as trained. Account for everyone. Call 911. Do not reset the panel or re enter until the fire department says it is safe. Report the event as a ticket afterward, and follow program incident reporting.

**Inspections and drills.** Facilities schedules and keeps records for all required inspections and tests of alarms, sprinklers, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and generators. Program staff run fire drills on the schedule licensing requires. Any finding from an inspection gets a ticket with a due date, and open findings past their due date appear in the monthly report.

**Records.** Inspection certificates, test records, drill records, and repair records are kept per licensing requirements and at least 7 years. These are among the first documents a surveyor asks to see.

## PRO-08. Accessibility and Adaptive Equipment

| Use this when | A lift, ramp, grab bar, door opener, or other adaptive feature is not working |
|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Every ticket in this category is at least urgent, and most are emergencies.** When adaptive equipment fails, a person loses access to their own bathroom, bedroom, or front door. Treat it that way.

**Report as an emergency**

1. A ceiling lift or portable lift that will not raise, lower, or hold.
2. A wheelchair lift or ramp that is not usable.
3. An accessible bathroom that cannot be used, including a roll in shower that will not drain or a raised toilet that is loose.
4. A powered door opener that has failed at a main entrance.
5. A grab bar, transfer pole, or railing that is loose. A loose grab bar is a fall about to happen.
6. An adjustable bed that will not move.
7. Any failure that means a person cannot get into or out of their home.

**Before you submit**

1. Stop using the equipment. Do not attempt a transfer with a lift that is behaving unusually.
2. Tell the nurse or supervisor so an alternative plan is in place for the person who uses it.

4. Note whether it is battery powered and whether the battery has been charging.
5. Photograph the equipment and any error light or code.
6. Say clearly in the ticket which person's daily routine is affected and what they cannot do right now. Do not include their name. Use the room or unit.

**Do not**

1. Do not repair, adjust, or improvise a fix on lifting or transfer equipment. These carry a person's full weight.
2. Do not substitute equipment that has not been approved for that person. Equipment is matched to the individual through the clinical process.
3. Do not remove a loose grab bar and leave the holes open.
4. Do not continue using equipment that makes a new noise, moves unevenly, or has a warning light.

**While it is out of service**

1. Tag it clearly so no one uses it.
2. The nurse or clinical staff set the temporary plan for the person affected.
3. If there is no safe alternative, tell the program on call number and the VP of Operations. This may become a relocation decision.

**Records.** Service records, inspection dates, and weight ratings for all lifting equipment are kept for the life of the equipment plus 7 years. Lifting equipment is inspected on the manufacturer's schedule, and those records are reviewed by surveyors.

|---|---|
| Submit to | Property Ticket Form: [insert Google Form link] |
| Owner | Director of Facilities and Fleet Management |

**Snow and ice**

1. Snow and ice removal is arranged in advance by Facilities for every site. Contractors are dispatched by forecast, not by call.
2. **Report an unsafe walkway even if the contractor has already been there.** A cleared path that has refrozen is more dangerous than uncleared snow.
3. Priority order for clearing is the main accessible entrance first, then other exits, then the walkway to the vehicle, then the parking area.
4. Every site keeps ice melt on hand before the season starts. If your site is low, submit a ticket in the fall, not in January.
5. If a person we support cannot leave the home safely, that is an emergency ticket and a call. A blocked accessible entrance is not a routine matter.
6. Report ice forming in the same spot repeatedly. That usually means a drainage or gutter problem that can be fixed permanently.

**Grounds**

1. Report tripping hazards on walkways, including uneven pavement, settled slabs, and loose stones.
2. Report loose railings on outdoor steps and ramps as urgent.
3. Report outdoor lighting that is out, especially at entrances and parking areas.
4. Report dead or damaged tree limbs over a walkway, a roof, or a parking area.
5. Report fencing and gates that do not close or latch.
6. Report standing water in a yard or near a foundation.

**Pests**

1. Report the first sighting. Do not wait to see whether it is a pattern. One report handled quickly costs far less than a treatment program.
2. Say what you saw, where, when, and how many times.
3. **Do not use store bought sprays, baits, traps, or foggers in a home.** Only licensed pest control applies treatment in a residence, and records of what was applied must be kept.
4. Keep food sealed, clean up promptly, and report gaps around pipes and doors that let pests in.
5. Bed bugs, cockroaches, and rodents are urgent, not routine, because they affect health and dignity and they spread.
6. Treatment in a home is scheduled with the program so that residents are not present during application and re entry times are followed.

**Records.** Snow contractor dispatch records, grounds inspections, and pest control treatment records including the product used are kept 7 years. Pest control records are reviewed during surveys.

## CON-01. Operating When Technology Is Down

| Use this when | Email, the internet, phones, or a computer system is unavailable |
|---|---|
| Owner | VP of Operations, with the Chief Operating Officer |

Care does not stop when technology does. This procedure covers how to keep working. TEC-05 covers reporting an internet outage. PRO-04 covers loss of power.

5. Call the program on call number if care documentation is affected.

| What is down | What to do | Who leads |
|---|---|---|

# Part 6. Management and Oversight

## MGT-01. Overseeing Aspen Technology

| Owner | Chief Operating Officer, with the Director of Finance |
|---|---|

**Why this matters.** Spectrum for Living has no internal information technology director. Aspen Technology performs the work, and the only way the agency knows whether it is being done well is to measure it. Without measurement, the agency is relying on the vendor's own account of its performance.

**What Spectrum keeps in its own hands**

1. The ticket sheet. Both Google Forms feed a sheet the agency owns. Aspen Technology works from it and updates it. **If tickets live only in the vendor's system, the agency has no record and no way to hold anyone to a response time.**
2. Administrator access to the agency's Google Workspace. Aspen Technology holds working access, but the agency holds an account of its own that it does not depend on the vendor to reach.
3. Ownership of the internet domain and the domain registrar account.
4. Copies of backups and the ability to restore without the vendor's cooperation.
5. The list of every system, its cost, its renewal date, and its administrator.

If any of those five sit only with the vendor, the agency cannot change vendors without disruption. Confirm all five before this manual is adopted.

**Quarterly review.** The Chief Operating Officer and the Director of Finance meet with Aspen Technology every quarter and score the following.

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| Met the first response time in the contract | 20 percent |
| Met the resolution time in the contract | 20 percent |
| Tickets reopened, or the same problem returning | 10 percent |
| Problems found by the vendor before staff reported them | 10 percent |
| Patching and updates kept current | 10 percent |
| Backups succeeded and restores were tested | 10 percent |
| Communication and responsiveness when escalated | 10 percent |
| Accurate invoices and no unexpected charges | 10 percent |

**Match the contract to this manual.** Every response time in this manual must be something the Aspen Technology contract actually requires. If the contract does not support it, either renegotiate or publish the time the agency can really meet. Publishing a response time the vendor is not obligated to meet creates an expectation that will be broken.

**Renewal.** Review the contract 90 days before it renews, against performance, current market pricing, and what switching would cost. Nothing renews without that review.

**Know the cost of leaving.** Write down what it would take to move to another provider: getting the data out, setting up a replacement, retraining, and any exit terms. A vendor whose exit cost is unknown has more power over the agency than their contract gives them.

**Escalate.** Missed response times two months in a row goes to the Chief Operating Officer with a written correction plan. A vendor security incident affecting agency information follows TEC-10 immediately.

**Records.** Contract, business associate agreement, quarterly scores, and escalations are kept 7 years after the relationship ends.

## MGT-04. Backups and System Recovery

| Owner | Chief Operating Officer, performed by Aspen Technology |
|---|---|

**A backup that has never been restored is a belief, not a protection.**

**Understand this first.** Google protects against its own equipment failing. It does not protect against a person or an attacker deleting or encrypting agency information. Google Workspace needs its own separate backup. That is a funding decision for leadership, listed in Part 9.

**Steps**

1. Agree with each system owner how much information the agency can afford to lose, and how long it can operate without that system. Proposals below require approval.

| System | Most that can be lost | Longest without it |
|---|---|---|
| Care documentation | 1 hour | 4 hours |
| Medicaid billing | 4 hours | 8 hours |
| Payroll and timekeeping | 4 hours | 8 hours |
| Google Workspace, email and files | 4 hours | 8 hours |
| Accounting | 24 hours | 24 hours |
| Ticket sheets | 24 hours | 24 hours |
| Donor database | 24 hours | 48 hours |

2. Back up on a schedule that meets those numbers.
3. Keep at least one copy off site that cannot be changed or deleted, including by someone with administrator access. **This is the specific protection that survives ransomware, because attackers go after backups first.**
4. Check backup jobs every day and fix a failed job within one business day.
5. Test a restore every quarter. Restore a single file, a full mailbox, and one full system. Write down what was restored, how long it took, and whether it was usable.
6. Run a recovery exercise once a year simulating the loss of a major system.
7. Confirm the agency meets the backup requirements in the cyber insurance policy.

**Escalate.** A failed restore test goes to the Chief Operating Officer immediately. A backup failure not resolved in 48 hours goes to the Chief Operating Officer. If the agreed recovery times cannot be met with current spending, the Chief Operating Officer decides in writing to fund it or to accept the risk.

**Records.** Job logs one year, restore tests three years, recovery exercises 7 years.

## MGT-06. Security Training and Phishing Tests

| Owner | Chief Operating Officer, with Human Resources |
|---|---|

**Steps**

1. Assign orientation training before an account is turned on, and security awareness training within 30 days of hire and annually after that.
2. Send test phishing messages [monthly for higher risk roles, quarterly for all staff].
3. Build the tests around what actually threatens this agency: vendor banking changes, executive impersonation, payroll redirection, messages that look like they came from a state agency, and shared file links.
4. Measure two numbers. The click rate is how many people clicked. **The report rate is how many people reported it, and it matters more.** An agency where people occasionally click but report within minutes is safer than one where nobody clicks and nobody reports.
5. When someone clicks, send a short explanation of the warning signs. Do not discipline.
6. If someone clicks three times in twelve months, the supervisor has a supportive conversation, assigns targeted training, and considers whether workload or the workstation is part of the problem. Discipline is considered only for refusing to complete required training, not for clicking.
7. **Never build a test message around bonuses, layoffs, benefits changes, or family emergencies.** Those work, and they destroy the trust that makes people report.
8. Use real incidents from the agency, with names removed, in training within 30 days of the event.
9. Provide training in accessible formats and in translation where needed.

**Escalate.** A department below 90 percent completion at 45 days goes to the director. A click rate above 10 percent or a report rate below 40 percent goes to the Chief Operating Officer and the program is redesigned.

**Records.** Completion records are kept 7 years after separation. Test results are kept three years.

## MGT-07. Technology and Property Change Control

| Owner | Chief Operating Officer for technology, Director of Facilities and Fleet Management for property |

## MGT-09. Replacement Planning and Budget

| Owner | Chief Operating Officer, with the Director of Finance |
|---|---|

**Purpose.** Replace equipment, building systems, and vehicles on a plan instead of in an emergency. Planned replacement costs less and disrupts residents less.

**Steps**

1. Build a five year replacement schedule from the actual inventory: computers and tablets from the device list, building systems from the site records, vehicles from the fleet list. Use the expected life in TEC-15 and the building system records.
2. **Level the schedule.** If one year carries far more than the others, move purchases forward or back. A one year spike is usually left over from a past bulk purchase and will repeat every cycle unless it is broken up.
3. Build the annual budget in four parts: ongoing costs such as Aspen Technology, licenses, internet, utilities, and service contracts; scheduled replacements; projects; and a contingency of 5 to 10 percent.
4. Report actual spending against budget every month.
5. Report the cost of unused software licenses every quarter and return those savings to the technology budget rather than surrendering them.
6. Evaluate every significant purchase on four things before features: what it connects to, the total cost over three years, what it would cost to leave, and whether the agency can get its information out. **At this size a platform choice is a commitment of five to ten years, and the cost of leaving is usually larger than the purchase price.**
7. Report every year how many devices, building systems, and vehicles are past their expected life. If that percentage rises year over year, the agency is accumulating a deferred cost, and the report should say so plainly.
8. Use the repeat problem reports from both ticket sheets. Anything with three or more tickets already has a documented case for replacement.
9. Because technology, buildings, and vehicles all report to the same executive, forecast them in one capital view rather than three.
10. Work with the Director of Development on grant opportunities for accessibility, energy efficiency, and safety improvements.

**Escalate.** More than 15 percent of any asset class past its expected life, a budget variance over 10 percent, an emergency replacement over [insert amount], or any platform decision where the cost of leaving has not been written down.

**Records.** The replacement plan, budget submissions, variance reports, and cost analyses are kept 7 years.

## QR-2. How to Spot and Report a Phishing Email

**Warning signs**

1. The message says it is urgent or has a deadline today.
2. It asks for money, gift cards, a banking change, or payroll information.
3. It asks you to sign in through a link.
4. The sender's address does not match the name shown, or is slightly misspelled.
5. There is an external sender banner on a message that claims to be from a coworker.
6. There is an attachment you were not expecting.
7. It says to keep the request confidential, or not to call.
8. The sender says they are in a meeting and cannot take calls.
9. A new phone number claims to be an executive.
10. Hovering over the link shows an address that does not match the text.

**What to do**

1. Do not click, reply, or open the attachment.
2. Do not forward it to coworkers to ask what they think.
3. In Gmail, click the three dots at the top right of the message and choose **Report phishing**.
4. Forward it to [insert address].
5. If it involves money or looks like it came from an executive, also call Aspen Technology at [insert number] and tell the Director of Finance.
6. Wait to be told it is safe before deleting it.

**If you already clicked or typed your password**

1. Call Aspen Technology at [insert number] immediately. Do not send an email.
2. Unplug the network cable and turn off the Wi-Fi.
3. Do not change your password yourself. They will reset it and sign the attacker out at the same time.
4. Tell your supervisor.
5. **You will not be in trouble. Reporting quickly is what protects everyone.**

**No executive at Spectrum for Living will ever ask you by email or text to buy gift cards, send a wire transfer, or send employee payroll information.**

## QR-3. What to Do If a Computer May Have a Virus

**Signs.** Files renamed or will not open. A note on the screen demanding payment. Sudden extreme slowness. Programs opening by themselves. Security software turned off. Network drives suddenly failing.

**Do these in order**

1. Stop typing. Do not click anything else.
2. Unplug the network cable and turn off the Wi-Fi.
3. **Do not turn the computer off**, unless there is smoke, fire, or a safety hazard. Turning it off destroys information needed to find out what happened.
4. Take a photo of the screen with your phone.
5. Call Aspen Technology at [insert number]. After hours call [insert number]. **Do not send an email**, because email may be affected.
6. Tell your supervisor and the site lead.
7. Tell coworkers out loud not to use shared drives until you are told it is safe.
8. Say right away if files on a shared drive are affected. That means it is spreading.

**Do not** run a virus scan, delete files, try to clean it up, or plug in a USB drive or phone.

**If the computer is needed for care documentation**, say so, and ask for a replacement. Do not keep using the affected computer.

## QR-7. Water Leak or Flood

**Call 911 first if water is near outlets, panels, or electrical equipment, or if anyone could be shocked. Do not walk into standing water that may be in contact with electricity.**

**Stop the water if you safely can**

1. Find the shutoff valve. Toilets and sinks have one behind or below them. Turn it clockwise, gently.
2. **Do not force a stiff valve.** An old valve can break and make the leak much worse.
3. If you cannot find or turn it, call Facilities at [insert number] immediately.
4. The main water shutoff location should be labeled at your site. If it is not, submit a ticket to have it labeled.

**Then**

1. Put down towels or a bucket and keep people away.
2. Photograph the leak and any damage.
3. Report it. Running water you cannot stop is an emergency. A slow drip is routine, but still report it, because a slow drip becomes a ceiling collapse.
4. Say whether the water is clean, gray, or sewage. **Sewage backup is an emergency** and the area stays closed until it is cleaned properly.

**Do not** use chemical drain cleaners, plunge an overflowing toilet before shutting the valve, remove a ceiling tile holding water, or use a household vacuum on standing water.

**Also report** a water stain, a musty smell, or peeling paint even with no active leak. Those are the early signs of a hidden leak.
